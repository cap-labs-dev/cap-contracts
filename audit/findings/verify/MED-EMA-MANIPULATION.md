## Verdict: CONFIRMED

## Why (one paragraph, the decisive reason)
The averaging window turns a free same-transaction manipulation into one that costs the time value of the parked capital for one window, and nothing more: `Stablecoin.deposit` is permissionless with no cap or pause (no `maxDeposit` override, not in the role table), mints at par with no fee, holds no yield and no exposure while `badDebt == 0`, and `maxRedeem` covers the whole park in the borrow block because the park raised `unlockedSupply` by exactly `D` (verified even with a pre-existing redemption queue that had already exhausted the reserve). The `mintAmount` plumbing only adds `L` to both sides of the averaged ratio, so it does not touch the `D` dilution; I recomputed the figures independently (9M/21M -> 7.679% -> 6,311 vs 9M/11M -> 10.909% -> 8,966) and they match the PoC to the cUSD. The depositor need not be the borrower or hold any role; the borrow needs the market's BORROWER role, but the borrower is precisely the adversary the NatSpec at `IInterestRateModel.sol:203-213` says the averaging defends lenders against ("cheaper for a borrower who suppressed it"), so the role is not a mitigation, it is the threat model. Loss is a direct, repeatable underpayment of stcUSD holders (the premium is minted to `stakedStablecoin` in `BaseMarket._chargePremium`), bounded per loan by the slope portion of the curve (max ~54% here, since the rate floors at `base`) and requiring capital of the order of the stablecoin's supply, which is a Medium under Immunefi-style scoring: third-party fund loss, bounded, needs a permissioned-but-adversarial counterparty and large-but-borrowable capital. The NatSpec's "standing behind an unwanted position ... exposed to everyone else" is false for the supply-dilution direction; it is only true for the credit direction (inflating utilization by borrowing costs floating interest for the window).

5. Design intent: `IInterestRateModel.sol:203-213` and `InterestRateModel.sol:223-226` explicitly claim the cross-block manipulation is costly because of an "unwanted position ... exposed to everyone else". For a cUSD deposit the exposure is (i) a bad-debt recognition inside the window (haircut on redemption via `_convertToAssets`) and (ii) other redeemers draining the reserve ahead of you; over 5 min to 1 day with `badDebt == 0` both are negligible. ## Corrections to the finding text (bullet list, or "none")
- The depositor holding no role is asserted but not demonstrated in the PoC (the attacker is `defaultBorrower` throughout); my verify test demonstrates it with a roleless `whale`. Worth stating explicitly since it means a borrower can have any third party carry the capital.
- State the bound: the fixed rate cannot fall below `base`, so the lender loss per loan is at most the slope portion of the honest premium (`~54%` on these slopes; `max discount (bps): 5365`). The 30% figure is the `D = S` point; the attack needs capital comparable to the stablecoin's supply, which is a real (if borrowable) precondition and worth putting in Likelihood explicitly rather than only "enough USDC to move utilization materially".
- The 10% opportunity rate is generous; at a ~5% Aave USDC borrow rate the cost for 1h on $10M is ~57 cUSD (multiple ~47x). A flash loan does not work (the window is at least 5 minutes, `MINIMUM_AVERAGING_PERIOD`), so the attacker needs a real short-term loan; that is the only capital friction.
- Add the side effect from `test_floatingRateAlsoDropsDuringTheWindow`: the park lowers the floating liquidity rate for the entire stablecoin for the window (10% -> 7% here), a further ~23 cUSD/hour transferred from lenders to all floating borrowers.
- The slopes used are the test deployer's (`CapDeployer.sol:111`, `applyLiquiditySlopes = false` by default; no deploy config sets slopes, governance sets them post-deploy). The shape of the result holds for any kinked curve, but the specific cUSD figures are on a hypothetical curve; say so.
- Recommendation (1) `max(average, spot)`: note the cost it imposes on honest fixed borrowers is transient (one window after a large withdrawal) and that it still leaves the floating-side side effect untouched, which is acceptable since floating reprices every move.

## Residual doubt (what would settle it if still uncertain)
Whether the protocol treats market BORROWERs as legally bound, trusted counterparties (offchain agreements, KYC) rather than adversarial users. If the team's threat model is that a borrower gaming their own rate is a contractual breach handled offchain, they may accept this as Low/acknowledged; but the code's own NatSpec names the borrower as the adversary the averaging exists to stop, so on the code as written Medium stands. The exact production slopes and averaging period would fix the cUSD figures but not the conclusion.
