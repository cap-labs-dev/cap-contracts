## Verdict: CONFIRMED

## Why (one paragraph, the decisive reason)
The arithmetic is exactly as stated and is a bug against the code's own semantics, not a design disagreement: `claimableRedeemRequest` (`contracts/ERC7540/ERC7540AsyncRedeem.sol:129`) measures each window against `settledQueue + unlockedSupply()`, `_withdraw` (`:241`) adds every settled share to `settledQueue` regardless of position, and the control case (B1b, no out-of-order claim) proves the intended semantics are "claimable = f(live unlocked supply)" — a fully-claimable request is re-locked to 0 when `unlockedSupply` falls — so "once claimable, always claimable" is *not* the model, and the over-credit `min(a, settled-behind)` at `unlockedSupply()==0` is simply wrong. Nothing downstream catches it: `Tranche._withdraw → _transferOut → Vault.transfer` is unconditional and there is no post-redemption health check anywhere on the tranche path. I reproduced it without any of the PoC's "everyone chose async while instant was open" shortcuts: both requests made while the tranche was fully locked (async is the only exit), unlocked by an ordinary borrower `repay`, the later request claimed first by a faster holder, re-locked by a price move that leaves the market healthy exactly at the buffer floor `lt/(lt−buffer)=1.1428` where `liquidate` reverts `Healthy`; the earlier claim then pays 200e18 through the lock, health falls to 0.914, and the production `LIQUIDATOR` role slashes 552e18 collateral units (69% of the remaining holder's position) that the buffer was designed to make untouchable. Unprivileged, no misconfiguration (production `buffer=0.1e27`, `DeployInfra.sol:89`), organic trigger, real loss to third parties, and at larger over-credits (≥~29% of the tranche at the floor) it becomes unrecoverable debt on cUSD. High stands. The attacker-profit framing of "exploit path B" is wrong, though (see corrections): it is dominated by instant exit and only pays if the actor also holds `LIQUIDATOR`; the realistic vector is the organic slow-claimant one, and the Underwriter's keeper-finalized `deallocateAsync` is the canonical slow claimant.

## Corrections to the finding text (bullet list, or "none")
- **Exploit path B (deliberate pre-credit) has no unprivileged motive and should be reframed.** A request that is fully claimable at request time could have been instantly redeemed for the same amount; queued shares stop earning and stay slash-exposed. The pre-credit is strictly dominated by exiting unless the actor also holds `CapRoles.LIQUIDATOR` (role-gated) to collect the bonus, in which case the profit is the bonus (2 % default) on the liquidation they induce. Cycling additionally requires the tranche depositor role. Present the finding as an organic accounting defect that loses third-party funds in normal operation (slow claimant, Underwriter keeper flow), not as an attacker-profit exploit.
- "**borrow**" as a re-lock trigger applies only to junior tranches. The credit limit excludes queued capital, so a borrow at the limit cannot re-lock a queued position in the most senior tranche; for the senior the trigger is price, accrual or junior slash.
- The junior PoC "drains the locked tranche" but does not move health (1.6 after the exit in my run); the harm there is first-loss shifting onto the senior, not liquidation. Worth saying explicitly so the two PoCs are not read as the same impact.
- Underwriter paragraph: add that `finalizeDeallocateAsync` sizes off `queuedRequest`, so the Underwriter as a *claimant* in a Tranche is the most likely organic instance of the exit-through-lock, independent of the misreport on its own queue.
- Loss statement can be sharper: in the organic case the whole liquidation is caused by the claim (the market was healthy and `liquidate` reverted `Healthy` one call earlier), so the remaining holders lose the full slashed collateral, not just the bonus; in my run 552e18 of 800e18 units.
- Minor: "at gas cost only" for path B should read "at gas plus forgone premium on `a` for the whole wait".

## Residual doubt (what would settle it if still uncertain)
Existence and unprivileged reachability are settled. The only open question is whether the team regards the buffer as a hard guarantee (in which case High, possibly higher once the Underwriter is the slow claimant and bad debt is reachable) or as a soft target with liquidation as the real backstop (in which case one could argue Medium: "risk-control bypass, loss borne by LPs, no attacker profit"). The `lockedValue` NatSpec and the `lt − buffer` sizing read as a hard guarantee, and the loss in my run is 69 % of the remaining holder's collateral, so I keep High. A statement of the Underwriter keeper's finalize cadence would sharpen likelihood but does not change the verdict.
