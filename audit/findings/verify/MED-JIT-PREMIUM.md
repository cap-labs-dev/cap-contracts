## Verdict: CONFIRMED

## Why (one paragraph, the decisive reason)
The 6-hour vest is documented as a "smoothing window ... a curator knob" (`contracts/cap/Registry.sol:379-381`), not as an exposure-attribution mechanism, and it does not do that job: `PremiumVesting` credits `perShare` to whoever is in `stakedSupply` while the lump releases, so any admitted depositor who enters at any point inside the 6h window after a fixed `borrow` takes a pro-rata share of the *remaining* release, then leaves in full because `unlockedSupply` only pins `totalDebt/(lt-buffer)` (0.7 under both test and production `DeployInfra.sol:88-89`). No front-running is needed (my back-run test: arriving 1h late still nets 5/12 of the lump and a 100% instant exit), the junior tranche exits just as freely, early repay does not refund the lump, and the 6h of slash exposure on a fresh ltv-0.5 loan is negligible against 120x the honest 6h yield. The `setVestingPeriod(term)` knob does cut the take to 41 bps of the lump, but fixed markets have variable terms and every `fund` restarts the epoch, so it is a blunt instrument rather than an existing mitigation. Medium is the right level: it is redistribution of not-yet-vested premium among admitted underwriters with capital genuinely at risk, no already-claimable balance is touched, and the per-event cap is one term's lump; not High because the attacker must be an admitted depositor and the loss is an incentive defect for honest long-term underwriters rather than extraction of settled funds.

## Corrections to the finding text (bullet list, or "none")
- "Carol front-runs the fixed borrow" / "pending `borrow` calls on a fixed market" as a signal: front-running is unnecessary. Any admitted depositor entering at time t inside the 6h vest takes (1 - t/6h) x pro-rata of the lump and exits in full; the borrow event itself is the signal, so private mempools/L2 sequencers do not reduce likelihood.
- Exploit path (b) step 3 is correct for the tranche path only. In path (a) (Underwriter) carol cannot instant-redeem (`maxRedeem` = 0 after auto-allocation); she collects the swept accrual but stays exposed until the curator deallocates. The (a) root cause should be stated as `Underwriter.totalAssets` omitting the tranche `claimable` receivable (ERC4626 share price excludes accrued yield), bounded by `report` cadence.
- The PoC's tranche0 is the senior tranche with an empty junior; premium reaches it through the `seniorActive` remainder branch. Worth stating, since it also works in the junior tranche (verified).
- The PoC's 500 borrow exceeds alice's solo credit limit (1000 x ltv 0.5 = 500, `InsufficientLiquidity` with the premium added); carol's deposit was needed for that size. Immaterial (400 reproduces with alice alone), but the text "Alice keeps 100% of the remaining risk" should note the market stays healthy after carol's exit precisely because `lockedValue` (726) < alice's 1000, which is the buffer working as designed.
- The remark that `fund` restarting the epoch lets "an actor keep a lump partially locked until their own capital is in place" belongs to the C8 Low, not here: the re-vested locked remainder goes to the holders who carry the corresponding future risk.
- Recommendation is sound; add that `setVestingPeriod(term)` is available today as a stopgap for single-term fixed markets (cuts the 6h take to ~41 bps of the lump) but is undermined by per-borrow epoch restarts (C8).

## Residual doubt (what would settle it if still uncertain)
Whether the protocol considers admitted underwriters a trusted set (in which case this drops to Low as an intra-cohort fairness issue). The Tranche NatSpec anticipating a public depositor role, and the absence of any exit lock tied to loan term, argue against that reading. Confirmation from the team of the intended `report` cadence would also bound path (a) precisely.
