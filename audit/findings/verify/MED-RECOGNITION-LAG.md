## Verdict: CONFIRMED

## Why (one paragraph, the decisive reason)
The par-exit window is real, reachable without any role, and not merely "the guardian was slow": `recognizeBadDebt` has exactly one caller chain (`BaseMarket._writeOff` <- `FloatingMarket.writeOff` / `FixedMarket.writeOff`, both GUARDIAN-only per `Registry._configureMarketRoles`), nothing auto-recognises on time, redeem, or index poke, and there is no pause on redemptions, so between the oracle tick that makes `unrecoverableDebt() > 0` and the guardian's transaction every redemption is paid par from the reserve. My Foundry branch test reproduces the model to the wei: on S = 100k, reserve 20k, shortfall 10k, a 10k par exit before write-off leaves the survivors' flat backing at 0.8889 instead of 0.9091, the exiting holder keeps 1,818 they would have been charged on the curve, and a surviving 10k redeemer receives 8,000 instead of 8,349 (-3.5%); aggregate value is conserved exactly (`A + totalAssets` identical across branches), so this is a redistribution onto survivors, which is precisely the "hand the loss to whoever is still holding" outcome the `_convertToAssets` NatSpec says the curve exists to prevent. Crucially the lag is structural rather than negligence: a write-off is permanent debt forgiveness (my third test: dip, write-off, price recovers -> borrower's debt 80k -> 70k with collateral untouched and health 1.83, `badDebt` 10k stays on cUSD holders forever), so a diligent guardian has a rational reason to wait out a dip before booking, and even a perfect guardian is at least one block behind a bot that redeems in the same block as the oracle update. That takes it out of the "requires a privileged role to misbehave" exclusion; Medium stands, though two of the recommendations must be struck (see corrections).

## Corrections to the finding text (bullet list, or "none")
- Strike the recommendation "a keeper-callable `writeOff` bounded exactly as today": write-off is permanent debt forgiveness with collateral untouched (step 6), so a permissionless trigger converts every transient oracle dip into a borrower windfall paid by cUSD holders. That is worse than the lag.
- The "provisional shortfall" recommendation (subtract `sum market.unrecoverableDebt()` in `_convertToAssets`) needs more than the flicker caveat: `_onWithdraw` only retires the haircut against `badDebt`, so a provisional haircut has nowhere to be booked and would strand in the reserve as a donation to survivors; and it makes the redemption price depend on every market's oracle on every call. If a fix is wanted, the safer shape is a guardian-set *provisional* flag / SLA, or a two-step write-off (mark, then confirm after N blocks) that prices redemptions off the mark.
- State explicitly that the transfer is aggregate-conserving (`A + totalAssets` identical across branches); the finding says "transfers" but "shifts $2M of loss" reads as a new loss. Also note the $2M is measured against the flat ratio; against the curve the early exiter's avoided haircut is larger (backing^2 at the margin).
- Add that the defaulting borrower is itself a holder of credit-backed cUSD and can drain the whole unlocked reserve at par pre-recognition (step 2), which is the sharpest instance of the window.
- Add the structural reason for the lag (write-off = irreversible forgiveness -> rational guardian waits), which is the argument that lifts this above "trusted-role inaction".
- Likelihood section: the informed holder does not actually need `unrecoverableDebt() > 0`; par exit is available on any bad news (health < 1 with no liquidator, 18.4% before unrecoverable at defaults). The finding's window is the sub-case where the loss is already certain on-chain, which is the right thing to scope but should be said.

## Residual doubt (what would settle it if still uncertain)
Whether the team regards the pre-recognition par window as an accepted property of a par-redeemable reserve (in which case the `_convertToAssets` NatSpec should say so and the design claim should be qualified to "after recognition"), and what the production `users.guardian` actually is: a hot-key bot reacting within a block makes the exploitable window seconds and the residual loss small; a multisig or timelocked guardian makes it hours-to-days and the $2M-class transfer realistic. Neither changes the verdict, only the magnitude.
