### [MEDIUM] `Stablecoin.invest` moves reserve underlying out of the contract without touching `unlockedSupply`/`maxRedeem`/claimable, so redemptions the contract has promised revert, and an Aera loss is borne 100% by the last redeemers as a hard zero instead of a haircut
**Location:** contracts/cap/Stablecoin.sol:L103-L118 (`invest`, `recall`); `unlockedSupply`/`_onWithdraw`/ERC7540 claimable rule (unchanged, still `balance`-blind)
**Impact:** (a) Liquidity DoS: with reserve R = unlockedSupply = $20M (S = $100M, u = 80%) and the round-1 5%/day redemption assumption, a one-day keeper recall latency means any invested fraction phi > 0.75 makes the first day of redemptions revert on `safeTransfer` (phi > 0.50 at u = 90%, R = $10M; phi > 0.25 at a 3-day latency). The revert is a bare ERC-20 transfer failure inside `redeem`/`claim`; `maxRedeem` and `claimableRedeemRequest` keep reporting the full amount. (b) Loss distribution: after a loss `ell` on the invested slice the contract holds `(1 - ell*phi)*R` but still promises `R`. Redeemers are paid par FIFO until the balance is gone; the last `ell*phi*R` of "unlocked" supply gets **nothing** (revert), not a haircut — e.g. phi = 0.5, ell = 20%: $18.0M paid at par, $2.0M (10% of the reserve) stranded; phi = 1, ell = 30%: $14.0M par, $6.0M stranded. Recognised as `badDebt` the same loss would be a 0.5–11.6% haircut spread monotonically along the queue (the convex curve the protocol built for exactly this). Nothing in `Stablecoin` can recognise an Aera loss (`coverBadDebt` only lowers `badDebt`; `writeOff` is a market action on borrower debt). (c) Keeping P(any revert within 7 d) < 1% under the round-1 run dynamics needs phi <= 0.76 of `(unlockedSupply - queue)` at 0.5%/day demand, **0.33 at 1%/day, 0 at >= 2%/day** with a 1-day recall; a standing $5M queue lowers each by a further 8–22% of R.
**Likelihood:** Requires only the KEEPER calling `invest` (its intended use) plus ordinary redemption demand or any drawdown in the Aera vault; no attacker and no capital. There is no on-chain cap: `invest(amount)` accepts anything up to `balanceOf(stablecoin)`, including the backing of shares already sitting in the redemption queue. Cost to trigger: zero beyond the keeper's normal operation.
**Exploit path:** (1) S = $100M, C = $80M, balance = R = $20M, queue empty; KEEPER `invest(15e6)` (phi = 0.75). (2) Aera vault marks a 20% loss (or simply has withdrawal latency); balance = $5M, `unlockedSupply()` = $20M, `maxRedeem` for every holder unchanged. (3) Holders redeem $5M at par; the $5,000,001st dollar reverts. (4) A holder who read the Aera position first exits at par in step 3; every later holder is either DoS'd (until `recall`) or, after the loss, permanently short $3M that no accounting line records. Net: first-out gets par, last-out gets zero — the round-1 M-2 first-mover problem re-created by a keeper action.
**Proof:** `audit/v2/models/reserve_investment.py` output below (parts a, b, c). A failing Foundry test (`invest(x)` then `redeem(unlockedSupply())` reverts while `maxRedeem` reports it) is owned by workstream N2 per the plan; the model gives the thresholds. If N2 cannot produce it, demote to Low.
**Recommendation:** Track an `invested` ghost and use `balanceOf(this) + invested` only for the reserve *identity* while gating instant redemption and the 7540 claimable rule on the actual `balanceOf(this)` (i.e. subtract `invested` from `unlockedSupply`'s instant portion), or require `balanceOf(this) - amount >= redemptionQueue + minLiquid` in `invest`. Give losses a recognition path (`recall` shortfall vs `invested` -> `badDebt`), so the convex haircut applies instead of a revert wall. Second-order: subtracting `invested` from `unlockedSupply` also lowers `utilizationRate` inputs' liquidity; check the IRM does not read `unlockedSupply`.
**Invariant broken:** I1 (`balanceOf(stablecoin) >= unlockedSupply` whenever `invested == 0` holds; the restated `balanceOf + invested >= unlockedSupply` holds only until the first Aera loss); I13 (`balanceOf(vault) >= redemptionQueue` fails after `invest(balance)`).

### [LOW] The 12 h exponential opt-in vest halves, but does not remove, the JIT take on a front-loaded fixed-market premium (round-1 M-3 partial)
**Location:** contracts/utils/PremiumVesting.sol:L27 (`VESTING_PERIOD = 12 hours`), L114-L123 (`optIn` sets `debt` at current `perShare`), L337-L340 (`_weight`); contracts/cap/market/FixedMarket.sol (`_chargePremiumForTerm`: whole-term premium minted at borrow)
**Impact:** A $10M 30-day fixed borrow at u = 90% mints $127,024 of liquidity premium into the Stablecoin pot at once. A depositor who opts in right after the borrow with capital equal to the honest opted-in stake (50% of `staked`) takes **19.7% of the pot for 6 h ($24,990), 31.6% for 12 h, 43.2% for 24 h, 49.1% for 48 h**, at an opportunity cost of $34–$6,849. Against a time-pro-rata share over the 30-day term that is 47x at 6 h, 26x at 24 h, 7.5x at 96 h; the JIT take never falls below time-pro-rata inside a 7 d or 30 d term (crossover exists only for a 1-day term, at 19.1 h). Round 1 was 50% for 6 h under the settable (6 h default) linear vest.
**Likelihood:** Permissionless (any cUSD holder; `optIn()` is free and `debt` correctly excludes pre-vested premium). Requires observing a fixed borrow in the mempool or block; cost is the opportunity cost of J for h hours. Honest opted-in holders lose exactly what the JIT takes.
**Exploit path:** (1) Observe `FixedMarket.borrow(L, maxTerm)` minting P to the pot. (2) Same block (after): deposit J = H at par, `optIn()`. (3) Hold 12 h; `claim()` receives 0.5·0.632·P = $40,147. (4) `optOut()`, redeem at par (no fee). Net $40,079 at H = J = $1M.
**Proof:** `audit/v2/models/run_dynamics.py` part C output below. No failing test (Low).
**Recommendation:** Stream the fixed-market premium into the pot over the term (fund per accrual, as the floating market effectively does) rather than the whole term at borrow; or make the vest time-constant scale with the funding term. Second-order: streaming defers lender yield recognition to the term.
**Invariant broken:** none (I19/I20 hold); this is a distribution property.

### [INFORMATIONAL] Liquidity premium vesting to opted-in cUSD holders does not change the reserve-ratio path; cashing vested premium against the reserve does, and non-opted-in holders are diluted by the full liquidity carry
**Location:** contracts/cap/market/BaseMarket.sol:L394-L397 (`fundCreditBacked` to the Stablecoin pot); contracts/utils/PremiumVesting.sol (`staked` denominator, zero-staked freeze)
**Impact:** With rho = 0 (premium held) the `R/S` trajectory is identical to round 1 for every opt-in fraction f ∈ {10%, 50%, 100%} (20% -> 15.5% at 1 y, < 5% at 4.5 y): minted credit-backed supply is minted either way and `unlockedSupply/totalSupply` does not see who holds it. If opted-in holders redeem every vested liquidity premium the day it vests (rho = 1), `R/S` at 1 y is 6.2% instead of 15.5% and the < 5% clock moves from **4.5 y to 1.1 y** (rho = 0.5: 11.5%, 1.7 y); redeeming the underwriter premium too empties the reserve in 188 d. This channel existed in round 1 (stcUSD -> cUSD -> redeem) and was not modelled; it is now one hop. Per-holder: a non-opted-in holder's share of supply falls 22.6%/yr at u = 80% (the full liquidity carry accrues to `staked` only); opted-in depositor APY on the liquidity pot is 52.5% at f = 100% of the $20M depositor float, 525% at f = 10% — but borrowers can opt in their own credit-backed cUSD and dilute that by up to 5x. f = 0 freezes the pot (`_accrue` early-returns on `supply == 0`), which is safe (no sweep) but leaves premium unattributed.
**Proof:** `audit/v2/models/reserve_decay.py` section 5 and headline below.
**Recommendation:** Document that opt-in is the yield switch and that premium redemption is a reserve drain; consider a redemption fee on premium-sourced cUSD or counting claimed-and-redeemed premium in the reserve-decay dashboard.
**Invariant broken:** none (reserve identity holds).
