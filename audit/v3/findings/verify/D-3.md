# D-3 verification — expired unpaid fixed loan on a healthy market cannot be liquidated

**Verdict:** CONFIRMED-AT-LOWER-SEVERITY (Medium → **Low**, design note). The mechanism and the keeper-path timelines are correct (248 / 186 / 992 days reproduced), but the finding's central impact claims — "no on-chain remedy", "capital locked ... with no on-chain remedy", "Day 248: LIQUIDATOR clears it", and "borrower ... keeps the principal with no penalty" as a consequence of this design — do not survive. There is a documented, immediate guardian remedy; the keeper path is worse than stated (it never fully clears); and the borrower's outcome is identical under the recommended fix.

## What I re-derived (all confirmed)

Parameters: `lt 0.8`, `buffer 0.1` (both from `DeployInfra.sol:172-173`, so genuinely deploy values), `ltv 0.7` (max allowed by `setLtv`: `ltv + buffer <= lt`; the market owner sets it), underwriter rate 0.2/yr, liquidity slopes unset (base 0). Capital $1000, max draw → debt 700 (borrower receives 688.6 principal + 11.4 upfront premium), `h0 = lt/ltv = 1.1429`, threshold 800.

- `liquidate` → `_liquidate` (`BaseMarket.sol:355`) gates on `healthiness() < 1e27` only; `expiry[id]` is read only in `borrowMore`, `extend`, `extendAdmin`. Confirmed.
- Each `extendAdmin(id, 30 days)` at `expiry + grace + 1s` charges `debt · (31d+1s)/365d · 0.2 = 1.6986 %` compounding (`_rollFromNow` adds arrears on top of term; `_extend` charges on the whole `debt[id]`). `700·1.016986^n > 800 → n = 8`; the 8th call lands at `30 + 8·(31d+1s)` → day 248, debt 800.97. Matches. Harness slopes at 41 % utilisation give `r_liq = 0.0757`, total 0.2757/yr → 6 calls, day 186. UW 5 % → 32 calls, day 992. All three rows check out.
- At harness-default `ltv 0.5` (`h0 = 1.6`): 28 calls, day 868 (my `test_keeperPath_defaultLtv05_timeline`).
- Leakage formula `r_liq·(1+b) + b·r_uw` per year of debt is right: over 4 rounds the tranches paid `b·(U − U_borrow) = 0.02·258 ≈ $5` more than an immediate liquidation would have cost. Negligible at deploy rates; ~8 %/yr of D with the harness slopes.

## Strongest attacks on the finding

**1. "No on-chain remedy" is false — GUARDIAN `setBuffer`/`setLt` clears the whole loan at expiry (landed).**
`BaseMarket.setLt` (`:89-96`) only requires `buffer < lt ≤ 1e27`, and the inline comment says *"Dropping below ltv is allowed (forces unhealthy)"*; `variableCreditLimit` NatSpec says the same. `setBuffer(0)` then `setLt(1)` makes `debtLiquidationThreshold ≈ 0`, so `maxLiquidatable = 1.25·D / (1.25 − ~0) = D`, capped at `min(debt, recoverableDebt)`. The LIQUIDATOR then repays the full loan debt in one call and slashes exactly `D·(1+b) = 714`; guardian restores `lt`/`buffer` in the same block. `test_guardianSetLt_clearsExpiredLoanAtExpiry`: day 30, repaid 700.0, slashed 714.0, residual 0. A modest `lt = 0.11` clears 96 % in one shot (`test_guardianSetLt_modestDrop_partialClear`). The lever is blunt (market-wide health for the duration of the window, every `extend`/`borrow` in that market reverts `Unhealthy` meanwhile) but safe: `liquidate` is LIQUIDATOR-gated, so nobody else can act in the window. Both roles are protocol-held, i.e. trusted under the brief's stance. So the protocol has a 5-tx immediate remedy today; what it lacks is a *loan-scoped* one.

**2. GOVERNOR `setTermLimits` + one `extendAdmin` crystallises at day 31 (landed).**
`_rollFromNow` bounds the requested term by `maximumTermLimit`, and the premium for the whole term is charged up front. `setTermLimits(365 days, 1 day)` then `extendAdmin(id, 365 days)` at `expiry + grace + 1` takes debt to 840.4 (`h = 0.952`) on day 31 (`test_governorTermLimit_keeperCrystallisesAtGrace`). It mints ~140 of phantom underwriter premium to the tranches (circular), so it is not free of side effects, but the 186–992-day timeline is a property of the *30-day* term limit, not of the design.

**3. Numbers error: at day 248 the liquidator does not "clear it" — the keeper path never clears (landed, and it cuts the other way).**
`maxLiquidatable` (`BaseMarket.sol:245-255`) targets `targetHealth = 1.25`, not the loan. At day 248 (debt 800.97, capital 1000) it returns `(1.25·800.97 − 800)/(1.25 − 1.02·0.8) = 463.6`; after that the market is healthy again (`h = 1.25`) and `liquidate` reverts. Each subsequent round needs the residual to grow 25 % (`ln 1.25 / ln 1.016986 → 14` rolls = 434 days) and clears 58 % of it:

| round | day | debt at liquidation | repaid | residual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 248 | 800.97 | 463.64 | 337.34 |
| 2 | 682 | 427.05 | 258.38 | 168.67 |
| 3 | 1116 | 213.52 | 129.19 | 84.33 |
| 4 | 1550 | 106.76 | 64.59 | 42.17 |

After 4.2 years 42 of debt is still open, locking `42/0.7 = 60` of the remaining 66 of collateral; the tranche is ~90 %+ locked throughout. The finding's "$817 slashed on day 248" should read "463.6 repaid / 472.9 slashed on day 248, then a geometric tail over years". This strengthens the case for the recommendation (a maturity liquidation must be sized `min(amount, debt[id], R)`, not by `maxLiquidatable`) but it is a correction, not a refutation.

**4. Borrower gain is not a property of this design (landed).**
A defaulting borrower keeps the 688.6 principal under *any* liquidation rule, including the recommended maturity trigger — the loan is unsecured from the borrower's side by construction. The premium that accrues after expiry is charged to a debt the defaulter never pays; it only redistributes between tranches and cUSD holders. So "Borrower net: +$700" and "walks away ... with no penalty" describe the product, not the bug. A *non-defaulting* late borrower with an active keeper pays exactly the contracted rate (no gain vs. a voluntary extension, which is market-owner-gated anyway); with an idle keeper they get free time, which the `repay` NatSpec already documents. The real borrower-side asymmetry is that a borrower can hold the loan open indefinitely by paying only the periodic premium and neither the owner nor the underwriters can call it (`test_borrowerCanRollForeverByPayingTheRate`: 372 days open, principal unchanged, senior `unlockedSupply` = 0). That is illiquidity at the contracted yield, not loss.

**5. Already-documented design + existing test (partial).**
`IFixedMarket.repay` NatSpec: *"Arrears accrue only when extend or extendAdmin runs after expiry; a keeper must call that after the grace period"*; `extendAdmin` NatSpec: *"Health is not checked potentially making loan liquidatable"*. `test/integration/AuditValidation.t.sol::test_audit_overdueRepaymentDoesNotSettleArrears` (a prior-round validation test) already pins "365 days late, debt unchanged, repay at original debt". So the accrual-via-keeper mechanism and the no-late-penalty behaviour are accepted design. What is *not* documented anywhere is the operational procedure for closing a defaulted loan (the guardian `setLt` sequence) or that the keeper path alone takes years and never fully clears. That gap is the residual value of the finding.

## Who is harmed, and is it loss or illiquidity?
Underwriters. Incremental *loss* versus an immediate maturity liquidation is `b·U + (1+b)·L`: ≈ $5 over 4 years at deploy rates (liquidity rate 0); ≈ 4 % of D over 186 days with the harness slopes (and production will have non-zero slopes, so the 186-day/8 %-per-year row is the realistic one). The dominant harm is *illiquidity*: `lockedValue = D/(lt − buffer)` locks 100 % of a max-drawn market's capital for the whole keeper timeline, and the tail keeps most of it locked for years — unless the guardian runs the `setLt` sequence, in which case the loss crystallises at `D·(1+b)` on day 30 exactly as the recommendation would. `writeOff` is not a substitute (bounded by `unrecoverableDebt`, which is 0 on a healthy market).

## Recommendation to the lead
**Demote to Low and re-word.** Keep it: the observation that the *intended* keeper path takes 186–992 days to reach liquidatability and — new — never fully clears because `maxLiquidatable` is health-targeted, is real and the maturity-trigger recommendation (loan-sized, `min(amount, debt[id], R)`, bonus reconsidered) is the right fix. But it is a documented design (`repay`/`extendAdmin` NatSpec, `setLt` "forces unhealthy" comment, existing `test_audit_overdueRepaymentDoesNotSettleArrears`), liquidation is already fully permissioned so "no permissionless remedy" is the status quo for every liquidation, and the protocol has an immediate guardian remedy (`setBuffer(0)`/`setLt(1)` → liquidate → restore) that crystallises the same `D·(1+b)` loss on day 30. Strike "no on-chain remedy", strike the borrower-gain framing (identical under the fix; principal received is 688.6 not 700), replace "Day 248: LIQUIDATOR clears it, slashing $817" with the partial-clear table above, note that the incremental economic loss at deploy rates is ~$5 on $700 over four years (illiquidity, not loss; ~8 %/yr of D with realistic liquidity slopes), and add the guardian sequence as the current operational workaround with its bluntness (market-wide window, phantom premium if done via term limits) as the reason a loan-scoped trigger is still worth adding.
