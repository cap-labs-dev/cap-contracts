# G-1 — Liquidity premium vests only to opted-in cUSD; a borrower that parks its loan opted-in earns more than it pays while staked/S < u/(1+uw/r)

**Verdict:** CONFIRMED — **Medium** (as rated). Mechanically exact, numerically conservative (on-chain profit is ~1.7x the model), and the launch case is worse than the finding states. Not High: the value captured is a transfer of yield the honest stakers were already receiving above the borrow rate (dilution only, principal untouched), it self-limits at φ*, it needs a whitelisted borrower with a drawn line held idle, and the pot is zero until GOVERNOR sets a liquidity curve. Not Low: at launch-like states (credit ≫ opted-in stake; the Wrapper seed is 1 cUSD) the profitable regime is the default, and a $1M loan takes ~$10.5M/yr.

## (a) Re-derivation

Per charge on a floating market (`FloatingMarket.sol:L222-226`), with scaled debt `s`, liquidity index `L`, underwriter index `U`:
`liq = s·(L₁−L₀)·U₀`, `uw = s·L₁·(U₁−U₀)`. Both are minted credit-backed. `liq` goes to `Stablecoin.fundCreditBacked` → `_mintCreditBacked(address(this), liq)` + `_fund(liq)` (L83-86); it then vests with a 12 h time constant and is divided by `staked` (L242), where `staked` is the sum of opted-in balances kept in step by `_update` (L206-215). `optIn` (L114-123) refuses only `address(this)` and `DeadShares.HOLDER`. Nothing reads `creditBackedSupply` anywhere on the vesting path: a borrowed cUSD and a deposited cUSD are the same share to the pot. (Footnote: if no tranche is eligible for the underwriter leg — `_earnsPremium` false because tranche depositors have not opted in — the uw leg also falls into the cUSD pot, L466-474. Underwriter-vault capital is opted in by `Underwriter.addTranche`, direct tranche depositors are not.)

Annualised at multiplier 1, with `C` = total credit-backed supply (all markets, including the attacker's `D`), `W` = other opted-in stake, `r = r(u)`, `u = C/S`:

- pot per year ≈ `r·C` (floating debt × index growth; fixed markets add their upfront term premium the same way, L381)
- attacker's share = `D/(D+W)`
- attacker's cost = `D·(r + uw)`
- **net cost = D·(r + uw) − r·C·D/(D+W)**, negative iff `C/(D+W) > 1 + uw/r` iff `φ_T ≡ (D+W)/S < u/(1 + uw/r) ≡ φ*`.

This is the author's threshold; at u 0.8 / r 10% / uw 20% φ* = 0.267, uw 5% → 0.533, uw 0 → 0.8 (rate_sweep.txt φ* table reproduced below). With uw = 0 the condition is simply *opted-in stake < credit*.

Three things the static model leaves out, all of which favour the attacker on-chain: (i) every premium (liq and uw) is minted credit-backed, so `u` drifts up through the year (0.81 → ~0.85 in scenario A) and `r` with it, for every borrower; (ii) the liquidity leg is charged on the uw-inflated debt (`U₀` factor, L223), a cross-term; (iii) claims land in an opted-in balance and compound the stake. Model +$257k → on-chain **+$437k** for the author's scenario.

## (c) Strongest attacks on the finding, in the brief's order

**(1) Precondition that cannot hold on HEAD — the pot is empty at production launch.** No deploy script sets liquidity slopes (`script/` has only the `CheckRoles` wiring for `setLiquiditySlopes`); `liquiditySlopes` defaults to zero, `_nextLiquidityRate` returns 0, the liquidity index never moves and `fundCreditBacked` is never called with a non-zero amount. So "at launch" is strictly "once GOVERNOR sets a curve". That is a re-wording, not a refutation: a zero liquidity rate is not a product.

**(2) A check elsewhere — none.** `optIn` is unrestricted; the Wrapper route (`Wrapper.deposit`, opted in at L46) is equivalent; there is no reserve factor, no cap on per-share vest rate, no fee recipient (rate_sweep header: protocol take = 0, confirmed in `BaseMarket._chargePremium`). Nothing distinguishes credit-backed shares in the vesting path.

**(3) Numbers — the author is conservative.** Static +$257k is +$437k on-chain (drift, cross-term, compounding, above). The threshold φ* holds on-chain in both directions (D vs E). The launch case is more extreme than stated: with only the 1 cUSD seed opted in, a **$1M** loan captures $10.55M of the $10.55M pot at a cost of $365k. One correction to the author's *fix*, not their numbers: vesting over the reserve-backed supply `S − C` gives a staker yield of `r·u/(1−u)`, which still exceeds `r + uw` whenever `u > (r+uw)/(2r+uw)` — 0.75 at r 10%/uw 20%, 0.60 at uw 5%, 0.50 at uw 0. That recommendation does not close the loop at the utilizations where it matters. Only a bound of the per-share vest rate at ≤ `r(u)` (pro-rata over `totalSupply`, or an explicit cap with the excess retained/burned/to treasury) does.

**(4) Severity — the borrower role is not what creates the value.** Test F: a plain holder with $5M of USDC and no borrower role earns 36.9% in the same state. The pot `r·C` is a fixed transfer from borrowers to whoever opts in; at low φ_T it is a windfall for any staker, and the design's "only opted-in balances earn" (PremiumVesting L15) is what makes staker yield `r·u/φ_T` unbounded above the borrow rate. The borrower's specific ability is to *scale* the position with no capital — mint at `r + uw` instead of buying the (limited) float at a capital cost — and to do so up to the credit line rather than up to the available float. Against "zero capital at risk": on-chain the attacker does repay everything from holdings and keeps +$418k, so the narrow claim is right; but the borrower must be a whitelisted (KYC'd) party, must hold `D` idle for the whole period (the credit line cannot also be used), carries an unsecured obligation to the underwriters, and the carry flips negative the moment stakers arrive (D: −$683k/yr) — with the exit being an ordinary repay. Against "dilutes genuine stakers": yes, but only down toward `r(u)` (never below it; B: 51.6% → 45.8%), and never in principal; the attacker's net profit plus the uw it pays to underwriters is the honest stakers' dilution loss (a transfer), plus a small increment because the attacker's `D` raises `u` and therefore `r` for b1 and b2 (a real cost pushed onto the other borrowers, ~0.5 pp here, that flows into the pot). Under the stance that borrowers are third parties this is a rational, repeatable, self-limiting extraction of stakers' excess yield through circular unsecured credit, with no insolvency and no principal loss — Medium. Not High because the take is bounded by the excess above `r(u)` and the same excess is open to any staker; not Low because φ_T < φ* is the default state until stcUSD adoption catches up with credit, un-opted float anywhere (DEX pools, wallets, the other borrowers' own drawn cUSD) lowers φ_T, and at a realistic uw of 5% φ* is 44-53%.

**(5) Existing tests.** `test/unit/utils/PremiumVesting.t.sol`, `test/unit/cap/Stablecoin.t.sol` cover opt-in mechanics; `test/integration/AccountingIntegrity.t.sol` covers the idle-window freeze. Nothing in `test/` combines a borrow with an opt-in or bounds staker yield against the borrow rate; no `grep borrow.*optIn` hit. The candidate invariant I41 ("lender yield per cUSD ≤ borrower liquidity rate") is the right one and currently fails by construction whenever `staked < C`.

## Recommendation to the lead

**Keep at Medium; re-word the root cause and fix.** Lead with the invariant rather than the borrower: *the liquidity premium is divided by opted-in stake, so staker yield `r·C/staked` has no upper bound and exceeds the borrow rate whenever `staked < C`; a borrower is the actor that can arbitrage it with no capital, up to its credit line, while `φ_T < u/(1+uw/r)`.* State the launch consequence plainly: with the 1 cUSD Wrapper seed as the only opted-in holder, the first borrower (or first staker) to opt in takes the whole pot — on-chain a $1M loan nets $10.2M/yr on $80M of others' credit. Note that production starts at r = 0 until slopes are set, and that the harness uw of 20% is punitive (rate_sweep §4 break-even ~2%); at uw 5% the threshold is ~50% of supply. Replace the "vest over `totalSupply − creditBackedSupply`" recommendation — it leaves yield `r·u/(1−u)` and is still exploitable above u ≈ 0.6-0.75 — with: vest the liquidity leg pro-rata over `totalSupply` (per-cUSD yield `r·u ≤ r`, un-opted share retained), or cap `perShare` growth at `r(u)` per staked share with the excess to a treasury/burn. Keep the underwriter-leg footnote (BaseMarket L466-474: with no eligible tranche the uw leg also lands in the cUSD pot). Numbers to quote: on-chain +$437k/yr on $5M (author's state), +$176k at uw 5% / W 40M, −$683k above φ*.
