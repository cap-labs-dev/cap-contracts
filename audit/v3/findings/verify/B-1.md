# B-1 verification — dust-request flood bricks the 3-arg ERC-4626/7540 claim path of any controller

**Verdict:** CONFIRMED (Medium stands, at the low end of Medium). The mechanism, the gas numbers and the "no consent" root cause all reproduce to the wei. Two parts of the write-up are wrong and one is understated: recovery is *not* one-request-per-tx (a victim sheds 300 gifts in one 15.5M-gas tx, at 30% of what the attacker paid), the tranche depositor role gives no protection at all (share transfers are ungated, so likelihood is identical on every vault type), and the case that actually holds this at Medium — a contract integrator written to the standard interface has no escape and its queued shares are frozen — is not in the finding.

## What I checked (HEAD a843c1d)

Read end to end: `contracts/ERC7540/ERC7540AsyncRedeem.sol` (`requestRedeem`, `transferRequest`, `maxRedeem`, 3-/4-arg `redeem`/`withdraw`, `_claimableShares`, `_claimFifo`, `_sortIds`, `_consumeRequest`), `contracts/cap/Tranche.sol::unlockedSupply`, `contracts/cap/market/BaseMarket.sol::lockedValue`, `contracts/cap/Stablecoin.sol::unlockedSupply`, `contracts/cap/Underwriter.sol` (`deallocate`, `deallocateAsync`, `finalizeDeallocateAsync`), `contracts/cap/Wrapper.sol`, `contracts/utils/PremiumVesting.sol::_update`, `contracts/interfaces/IERC7540Redeem.sol` / `IERC7540AsyncRedeem.sol` NatSpec. Ran the author's PoC (`audit/v3/tests/scratch/B/B_P2_RequestFlood.t.sol`) mentally against my own re-measurement; my numbers match its table exactly at every shared point (n=300 reverse, 2 tranches: 31,164,493 in both).

## Strongest attacks on the finding, and why they did or did not land

**1. "Tranche and underwriter shares are behind a depositor allowlist, so the attacker needs a role."** Did not land. `deposit`/`mint` are `restricted`, but the share token is a plain ERC-20: `test_b_tranche_nonDepositorCanFloodViaShareTransfer` has a stranger with no role (deposit reverts `AccessManagedUnauthorized`) receive 1,000 wei of senior shares from any holder and plant 1,000 dust requests on the victim. On cUSD, 1,000 wei of underlying buys 1,000 wei of cUSD and does the same (`test_b_stablecoin_permissionless_oneWeiDeposit`). Likelihood is therefore the same on every vault type: "hold n wei of shares", and n wei is free.

**2. "The victim can only shed one request per tx (73k each), so recovery is as expensive as the attack."** Refuted. `transferRequest` is callable by an operator, and `setOperator` is one call. A 20-line helper contract (`Shedder` in my test) moves every gifted id in one tx: **n=300 → 15,462,316 gas, 51.5k per id, 29% of the attacker's 51.78M** (`test_d_victimShedsAllInOneTx_viaOperator`). A contract victim can do the same loop itself. After the shed, `maxRedeem` is 38.7k and the 3-arg redeem 134k. The victim needs the ids, which are only in `RedeemRequest`/`TransferRequest` events (indexed by controller) — an off-chain step, not a cost. Ping-pong is a losing game for the victim (`victimBack` 66.9k vs `attackerAgain` 47.0k), but sending to `0xdead` (72.9k) ends it; nobody can move a request off a controller that never calls `transferRequest`. So the attacker/victim gas ratio is 3.3:1 (reverse trick) or 2.3:1 (direct flood), not the 1.8–2.4:1 implied by "73k per transferRequest".

**3. "The 4-arg claim and the instant path are always available, so nothing is denied."** Partially lands, and it is what keeps this from being High. `test_c_escapeHatches_flatUnderFlood`: with 600 gifts, 4-arg `redeem` costs 95.6k and `instantRedeem` 38.5k; both flat. `claimableRedeemRequest(id, controller)` is a single storage walk, no loop. **But** the 4-arg claim, `transferRequest` and `setOperator` are all Cap extensions (`IERC7540AsyncRedeem`), not ERC-4626/7540. ERC-7540's own claim function *is* the 3-arg `redeem(shares, receiver, controller)` gated by `maxRedeem(controller)`. An integrator contract written to the standard (a 4626 router, an aggregator, any "async vault" adapter) that holds the shares as controller has neither `transferRequest` nor `setOperator` nor the 4-arg selector in its bytecode; once it is flooded past n ≈ 290 its queued shares are not merely inconvenienced, they are unreachable until the contract is upgraded. `instantRedeem` cannot rescue them either: the shares already left the controller's balance at `requestRedeem`. This is the concrete, non-fund-loss-but-fund-freeze case that justifies Medium, and the finding should say so instead of "the victim keeps the 4-arg escape".

**4. "Nothing in production calls the 3-arg path, so there is no victim."** Lands for the protocol's own contracts, not for the finding. `Underwriter.finalizeDeallocateAsync` uses the 4-arg claim keyed on its own `queuedRequest[tranche][id]` and its NatSpec explicitly acknowledges the gift surface ("Anyone can name this vault as the controller of a redemption they request against their own shares … the gift simply ignored") — the team knew controllers can be imposed and defended only the accounting, not the enumeration cost. `Underwriter.deallocate` uses `instantRedeem`. `Wrapper` is a plain ERC-4626 over cUSD that only calls `claim` on the vesting side and never touches the Stablecoin's redeem queue. So victims are external holders and integrators of cUSD, tranche and underwriter shares — exactly the population that would use the standard path. The finding already says this; I confirm it.

**5. Precondition / existing check.** None missing, none blocking. `requestRedeem` checks allowance on `_owner` only (`_checkAllowance(_owner, msg.sender, _shares)`), `_controller` is only checked non-zero; `transferRequest` checks the *current* controller only. `controllerRequests[c]` is an unbounded `EnumerableSet`. `test/unit/ERC7540/ERC7540AsyncRedeem.t.sol` covers `transferRequest` authorisation and a 3-request FIFO; nothing covers many requests, a foreign controller, or gas.

## Corrected / re-derived numbers (my run, HEAD, OZ 5.7.0)

Victim's gas after a flood of n dust requests (victim's own 100e18 request queued *after* the flood), 2-tranche senior with debt, `lockedValue` walk live:

| Path | n=100 | n=300 | n=450 | n=600 | crossing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| reverse-transferred (author's trick): `maxRedeem` | 1,946,402 | 5,761,967 | 8,623,744 | 11,485,610 | ~1,570 for 30M |
| reverse: 3-arg `redeem` | 6,650,651 | **31,164,493** | 59,630,797 | 96,737,892 | **≈ 290** (matches author) |
| reverse: 3-arg `withdraw` | 6,650,769 | 31,164,616 | 59,630,928 | 96,738,033 | ≈ 290 |
| direct `requestRedeem(1, victim, atk)` (ascending): 3-arg `redeem` | 4,723,819 | 13,864,261 | — | 27,577,560 | ≈ 650 (matches) |
| 4-arg `redeem` (escape) | 95,597 | 95,605 | 95,617 | 95,631 | flat |

Same flood on cUSD: 3-arg `redeem` 3,845,014 / 22,851,248 / 47,186,847 / 80,163,236 at n=100/300/450/600 → crossing ≈ 350 (matches). Underwriter shares: 4,218,070 / 23,963,650 at n=100/300 → crossing ≈ 340. Victim queued *before* the flood: within 0.2% of the "after" column (the sort dominates, not FIFO position); 4-arg 116k.

Attacker cost per id: 121.0–121.7k `requestRedeem` + 50.8–51.5k `transferRequest` (reverse) = ~172k; 121k direct. Reaching the 290 crossing ≈ 50M gas; on the L2/alt-L1 targets in `foundry.toml` that is cents. All as stated.

Decomposition at n=600 (reverse − direct ≈ 69M) shows the insertion sort is ~70% of the cost at the crossing; the per-id `unlockedSupply()` re-computation (`maxRedeem` + `_claimFifo`, each one `lockedValue` walk + one oracle staticcall per junior) is the remaining ~30% and is what makes the *direct* flood cross at 650 even with a linear sort. Both of the author's first two fixes are therefore necessary; fix (1) alone leaves a ~290→~650 crossing with the reverse trick still available, fix (2) alone removes strangers but leaves the O(n²) sort for a controller's own many requests.

Recovery: batched shed via operator 51.5k/id; single `transferRequest` 66.9–72.9k; single 4-arg zero-value claim ~95.7k.

## Recommendation to the lead

**Keep at Medium, re-word.** (i) Replace "must shed each gift individually: 73k per transferRequest" with "sheds in bulk through an operator/contract at ~51.5k per id, 29–43% of the attacker's spend, after recovering the ids from events"; the griefing ratio is 2.3–3.3:1 against the attacker, which is why this is not High. (ii) Delete the tranche/underwriter "needs a depositor" qualifier from Likelihood — share transfers are ungated, 1,000 wei of any share token suffices, so likelihood is uniform across cUSD, tranche and underwriter shares. (iii) Add the case that carries the Medium: a contract integrator coded to ERC-4626/7540 only (no `transferRequest`/`setOperator`/4-arg claim in its bytecode) has no escape, and its queued shares are frozen until it is upgraded; `instantRedeem` cannot help because queued shares have already left its balance. (iv) Cite `Underwriter.finalizeDeallocateAsync`'s NatSpec as evidence the imposed-controller surface is known and only the accounting side was defended. (v) On fixes: keep (1)+(2) as "both required" — my decomposition shows the sort is ~70% and the per-id oracle walk ~30% of the cost at the crossing, and either alone leaves a sub-1,000 crossing. Fix (2) as written (`isOperator(_to, msg.sender)`) would also break the Underwriter's own legitimate pattern of receiving requests it did not make only if some flow relies on it; none does today (it names itself controller), so it is safe.
