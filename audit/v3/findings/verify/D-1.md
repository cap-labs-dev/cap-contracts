# D-1 verification — one dead feed on a funded, staked tranche bricks floating `repay`

**Verdict:** CONFIRMED (Medium holds)

## What I checked (HEAD a843c1d, OZ 5.7.0 in node_modules)

Code path, read end to end:
`FloatingMarket.repay` (L75-80) → `_chargePremium()` (L170-185) → `BaseMarket._chargePremium(liq, uw)` (L435-475) → for every tranche `_earnsPremium` (L482-485) → `IPremiumVesting.stakedSupply() == 0 ? false : ITranche.totalCapital() > 0` → `Tranche.totalCapital` (L177-181) → `getPrice()` (L216-219) → `revert InvalidPrice()` when `Oracle.price()` returns 0 (empty chain, stale primary+secondary, staticcall failure, non-64-byte return, `≤0` answer via the adapter).

The only short-circuits before the price read are: same block as the last charge (`lastPremiumUpdate == block.timestamp`), `scaledDebt == 0`, `underwriterPremium == 0`, or the tranche having `stakedSupply() == 0`. The loop does not skip zero-weight tranches (weight is applied *after* `_earnsPremium`), and it prices the senior tranche too.

Author's PoC reproduces as stated (`FOUNDRY_TEST=audit/v3/tests/scratch/D`): 7 pass, `test_FAIL_borrowerCanRepayDuringOutage` fails with `InvalidPrice()`.

## Strongest attacks on the finding, and why they did not land

1. **Precondition that cannot hold?** The revert needs the dead tranche to have `stakedSupply > 0`, i.e. at least one holder has called `optIn()`. This is the normal state for a tranche that earns premium (the deployer's `_fundTranche` opts in; that is what depositors come for). Holds. The finding already states the condition ("whenever the dead tranche has opted-in shares"); it should be promoted to a hard precondition in the title/impact, and the round-2 "harmless" list should read *empty, debt-free, or unstaked*.

2. **A check elsewhere that blocks the path?** None.
   - Market owner setting `setUnderwriterRate(0)` does not zero the already-accrued premium: `IRM.updateUnderwriterRate` snapshots `index = underwriterIndex(market)` before changing the rate, so the next `_chargePremium` still sees `underwriterPremium > 0` and prices the dead tranche (my test 3).
   - Market owner cannot drop the tranche: `setTranches` is REGISTRY-only (`Registry._configureMarketRoles` L436-437); `setTrancheWeights` and `Registry.createTranche` both re-run `_setTranches → healthiness()` which walks `totalCapital()` and reverts (my test 5). Zero weight does not help (my test 6).
   - `setSource` is GOVERNOR-only (`Registry` L408). Confirmed.

3. **Numbers.** No load-bearing arithmetic in the finding. The `h = 0.816` figure is illustrative and not derived from the PoC scenario (PoC starts at h = 700·0.8/400 = 1.4); harmless but could be dropped.

4. **Severity.** The one thing the finding omits is a **non-GOVERNOR partial mitigation**: the dead tranche's stakers can `optOut()` (or transfer their shares to a non-opted account). `optOut` runs `_accrue` only — no price is consulted — and once `stakedSupply == 0`, `_earnsPremium` returns false before pricing, so `repay`, `chargePremium` and `setMarketMultiplier` work again (my test 4). This does **not** restore `borrow`, `liquidate`, `writeOff`, `healthiness`, or `unlockedSupply` of the dead tranche and everything senior to it — those walk `totalCapital()` directly. It requires cooperation from third-party depositors of the dead tranche, whom the borrower cannot compel, and it costs them their share of the premium that then gets distributed. So the borrower is *not* strictly locked to a GOVERNOR transaction to stop accrual, but has no path of their own. Under the stance that borrowers/depositors are untrusted third parties, this does not move the rating: the liquidation brick during the exact window that needs it, senior-depositor exit lock, and forced accrual on a debt that cannot be repaid remain. No direct theft, recoverable by one governor tx, no attacker needed but a realistic trigger (stale long-tail feed, retired aggregator, sequencer outage) → Medium is the right rating. Not High (no loss of principal; governor can unbrick), not Low (liquidation and exits blocked market-wide by one asset, and the new repay brick is a regression from what round 2 verified).

5. **Existing tests.** `test/integration/Tranche.t.sol::test_outstandingDebtStillRequiresAPrice` and `test/integration/DebtLifecycle.t.sol::test_zeroPrice_revertsInsteadOfDividingByZero` encode fail-closed pricing as *intended* for `totalCapital`/`unlockedSupply`, and `test_killedTrancheRemainsInTheMarket` documents that owners cannot remove a tranche. Nothing in `test/` exercises `repay`, `chargePremium` or `setMarketMultiplier` under a dead feed. The repay brick is untested and there is no comment suggesting it is intended; the `_earnsPremium` NatSpec ("shares survive a wipeout") is an asset-existence concern, which supports the author's fix (1).

## Recommendation to the lead

**Keep at Medium.** Re-word: (a) state "with opted-in (staked) shares" as a hard precondition of the repay/chargePremium/setMarketMultiplier brick, alongside "funded" and "market has debt"; (b) add that stakers of the dead tranche can `optOut` to restore `repay`/`chargePremium` without GOVERNOR — a third-party, partial mitigation that does not restore liquidation, write-off, borrow or senior exits, so it does not change the rating but the "GOVERNOR-only recovery" sentence is too strong for the repay leg; (c) drop the unexplained `h = 0.816`. Fix recommendation (1) — `_earnsPremium` should test `totalAssets() > 0` — is the correct minimal fix and matches the function's own NatSpec intent; I would present it as the primary fix and keep (3) (last-known-price fallback for liquidation) as optional with its stated risk, since it widens the oracle trust surface.
